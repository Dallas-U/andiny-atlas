# Persona Summary

The Super Administrator is responsible for the overall governance, configuration, security, and operational health of the Andiny Atlas platform.

Unlike other users, the Super Administrator is concerned less with individual investigations and more with ensuring that the platform operates securely, efficiently, and in accordance with organizational policies.

Their responsibilities span user administration, access control, system configuration, operational oversight, audit compliance, and platform maintenance.

---

# Primary Objectives

The Super Administrator aims to:

- Maintain platform security.
- Ensure uninterrupted system availability.
- Manage organizational users and permissions.
- Monitor operational health.
- Enforce governance and compliance.
- Review audit activities.
- Configure global system settings.
- Support organizational administrators.

---

# Core Responsibilities

The Super Administrator is responsible for:

- Creating and managing organizations.
- Creating Administrator accounts.
- Managing system-wide roles.
- Managing permissions.
- Monitoring platform usage.
- Reviewing audit logs.
- Monitoring system health.
- Managing security policies.
- Managing authentication policies.
- Monitoring application errors.
- Maintaining data integrity.
- Performing operational maintenance.
- Supporting disaster recovery procedures.

---

# Daily Workflow

## Morning

- Review overnight alerts.
- Review audit events.
- Check system health.
- Review pending administrative requests.

## Decision PD-004

### Title

**Exception-Driven Operational Oversight**

### Decision

Supervisory workspaces within Andiny Atlas will prioritise exceptions, risks, delays, pending reviews, and unresolved actions over general activity totals.

The system will proactively surface investigations and tasks that deviate from expected progress rather than requiring Supervisors to manually inspect every record.

### Rationale

Supervisors create the greatest value when they intervene in work that is delayed, blocked, incomplete, high-risk, or awaiting a decision.

Presenting every investigation with equal visual priority would create unnecessary cognitive load and make urgent issues harder to identify.

An exception-driven workspace enables faster intervention, improves workload management, and reduces the likelihood that operational risks remain unnoticed.

### Design Implications

This decision means that future Supervisor screens should include:

- Prioritised attention queues.
- Overdue and approaching-deadline indicators.
- Pending-review counts.
- Workload imbalance warnings.
- Escalation visibility.
- Risk-based sorting and filtering.
- Clear ownership and next-action indicators.

Routine work should remain accessible but should not compete visually with items requiring intervention.

## Decision PD-007

### Title

Read-Optimized Workspaces

### Decision

Not every operational workspace should prioritize creating or editing information.

Certain personas—including Auditors and Compliance Officers—will primarily consume, inspect, validate, and review information.

Their workspaces will therefore prioritize information visibility, traceability, filtering, and historical context over data entry.

### Rationale

Enterprise systems often optimize for data creation while overlooking users responsible for governance and compliance.

Review-centric users require rapid access to historical records, audit trails, evidence timelines, and investigative context.

Their workflows should minimise navigation while maximising confidence in the integrity and completeness of information.

# UX Considerations

The Auditor interface should emphasise:

- Traceability.
- Transparency.
- Historical context.
- Powerful filtering.
- Timeline visualisation.
- Confidence in data integrity.

The Auditor should be able to reconstruct the complete history of an investigation without unnecessary navigation.

---

# Persona Goals

Within the first minute after login, the Auditor should be able to answer:

1. What requires review today?
2. Are there any compliance concerns?
3. Can every investigative action be traced?
4. Which investigations require deeper inspection?
5. Are organisational procedures being followed?

---

# Experience Statement

The Auditor workspace should feel like opening an investigative history rather than a reporting system.

Every important action should be discoverable, traceable, timestamped, and attributable to the responsible user.

Nothing important should ever feel hidden.
